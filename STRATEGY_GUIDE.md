# Strategy Guide

Minimal Bastion rewards route coverage, counter timing, and completed upgrade roles more than raw tower count. A strong defense normally starts with inexpensive overlapping fire, adds control and armor/shield answers before those threats become dominant, and only then invests in support fields and remote coverage.

## Core principles

1. **Buy useful firing time.** A tower covering two nearby route segments is usually worth more than the same tower covering one straight segment. Corners, switchbacks, and parallel lanes are the premium positions.
2. **Build a functional opening before specializing.** Three or four well-positioned Needle Turrets are a reliable foundation. Add Frost, Shard, or another immediate counter before spending the entire reserve on upgrades.
3. **Complete counter roles on schedule.** Armor and shields punish a defense made only of many small hits. A tier-three Breaker or Prism is often more valuable than several additional tier-one generalists.
4. **Use control to multiply the whole defense.** Slow and stun create more firing time for every tower that covers the affected route. Arc Relay no longer receives a hidden damage bonus against slowed enemies; Frost and Arc still combine naturally through extra time on target.
5. **Do not stack Signal Beacons.** Overlapping auras use the strongest applicable value rather than adding together. Place a Beacon only when it supports a meaningful cluster, then put later Beacons on separate clusters.
6. **Treat targeting as part of the build.** Frost should usually stay on Fastest, anti-armor fire should use Armored, and priority damage should use Strongest or Support when signal carriers matter.
7. **Spend before the reserve becomes dead weight.** Complete doctrines and final roles, improve weak coverage, then use remote Watchtower/Mortar positions. Waves 21–30 unlock Apex promotions and are balanced around reinvesting in the established defense.

## Tower guide

### Needle Turret — economical coverage

Needle is the best opening filler and remains useful wherever several firing windows overlap. Its low price makes it ideal for learning a route, but small hits lose efficiency against armor unless upgraded appropriately.

- **Quick Feed → Ricochet Array:** crowd coverage. Use where grouped enemies remain close enough for the secondary hit.
- **Precision Feed → Piercing Rail:** armor-aware direct fire. Use on long approaches or against durable targets.
- **Targeting:** First by default; Armored can be useful for Piercing Rail.
- **Late game:** keep efficient, well-positioned Needles. Replace isolated low-impact copies when the same footprint can support a completed Breaker, Prism, or another missing counter.

### Frost Spire — route control

Frost is strongest where its area can repeatedly touch a busy lane. It defaults to Fastest so it distributes slow toward enemies most likely to escape instead of repeatedly choosing an already-controlled front target.

- **Deep Chill → Permafrost:** maximum slow area, strength, and duration. Best when several other towers exploit the added firing time.
- **Frost Bolt → Hail Barrage:** more direct damage with lighter control. Best when the defense already has enough slowing.
- **Targeting:** Fastest is the general choice. Support is situational in Signal Gauntlet if the carrier is the immediate danger.
- **Pairing:** any damage tower benefits from Frost. Arc gains no special damage multiplier; the combination is useful because both affect groups and layer slow with stun.

### Shard Fan — close crowd coverage

Shard Fan converts a good close-range corner into efficient multi-hit pressure. Poor placement hurts it more than most towers because both range and firing angle matter.

- **Wide Scatter → Shard Barrage:** broad crowd clearing on bends and long dwell zones.
- **Focused Shards → Piercing Fan:** tighter, harder volleys against armor and narrow lanes.
- **Targeting:** First normally keeps the fan aimed into the leading pack.
- **Placement:** put it close enough that several pellets connect; do not spend a premium central position on a route it only clips.

### Watchtower — remote priority fire

Watchtower turns distant build space into useful coverage and reaches across awkward geometry. Its value is access and target selection, not raw crowd throughput.

- **Tracking Optics → Rapid Sentinel:** broad long-range coverage and more frequent shots.
- **Heavy Shot → Piercing Sniper:** maximum reach and focused durable-target pressure.
- **Targeting:** Strongest; Support is valuable in Signal Gauntlet.
- **Placement:** preserve scarce near-route positions for short-range towers. Watchtower is designed to work from otherwise weak footprints.

### Ember Coil — sustained damage

Ember is a delayed-output tower: the burn continues after impact, so its contribution is less obvious than a direct burst. It is strongest when enemies survive long enough for the burn to tick without excessive overkill.

- **Quick Kindling → Wildfire Splash:** frequent group ignition and wide sustained pressure.
- **Intense Flame → Piercing Burn:** durable-target burn, range, and armor pierce.
- **Targeting:** First for spreading pressure through the route; Strongest for the single-target branch.
- **Pairing:** control helps enemies remain inside Ember coverage long enough for repeated applications.

### Breaker Cannon — armor and shield counter

Breaker should arrive before armored groups become the run's bottleneck. Armor Break also increases the effectiveness of subsequent small hits from the rest of the defense.

- **Rapid Breech → Armor Shatter:** frequent, four-target armor reduction for mixed armored packs.
- **Heavy Barrel → Piercing Round:** homing, tightly capped heavy hits for armored elites and bosses.
- **Targeting:** Armored is usually best; Support in Signal Gauntlet when carriers are present.
- **Planning:** one completed Breaker can unlock the damage already present in many Needle, Shard, or Arc attacks.

### Signal Beacon — non-stacking cluster support

Beacon is an investment in other towers. It does not attack and should not be purchased until a useful cluster already exists.

- **Field Amplifier → Rapid Field:** strongest attack-rate support in a medium field.
- **Expanded Field → Extended Field:** widest aura and largest tower-range increase.
- **Placement:** count the recipients and their quality. Four strong towers is a useful benchmark; supporting six weak or badly positioned towers does not make them efficient.
- **Overlap:** a tower uses the strongest Beacon values affecting it. Two overlapping Beacons do not add their bonuses, so separate their coverage.
- **Surge Nodes:** combat towers usually deserve the node footprint first. A Beacon just outside the node can support the cluster without consuming a boosted combat position.

### Arc Relay — connected-group damage and stun

Arc Relay is strongest against dense groups where each chain can find another target. It is weaker against isolated enemies and needs other focused damage for bosses.

- **Forked Arcs → Chain Storm:** maximum chain count and group throughput.
- **Charged Arc → Stun Coil:** fewer, harder arcs with substantially longer control.
- **Targeting:** First for stable chain origins; Support in Signal Gauntlet can remove carrier effects earlier.
- **Balance:** chain count is valuable only when enough enemies are in range. Seven theoretical arcs do not guarantee seven hits against sparse groups.

### Siege Mortar — predictive capped splash

Mortar uses remote space and fires toward predicted enemy movement. Each impact has a target cap, so extreme crowd density does not produce unlimited damage.

- **Quick Loader → Rapid Salvo:** frequent predictive shells with a compact blast.
- **Rangefinder → Shockwave Shell:** a wider ten-target blast with slow.
- **Targeting:** First normally leads the densest forward group; Support is useful against visible carriers.
- **Placement:** use distant footprints with long route access. Avoid relying on Mortar as the only answer to fast enemies because travel time and changing slows can alter the intercept.

### Prism Beam — shields, durable targets, and Expose

Prism applies focused pressure and Expose, increasing incoming damage from the entire defense. It is expensive and should usually follow a functioning opening.

- **Rapid Beam → Split Beam:** faster pressure and a second nearby target.
- **Amplified Beam → Shield Piercer:** maximum focused damage, pierce, and shield bypass.
- **Targeting:** Strongest; Support in Signal Gauntlet when a carrier is enabling a dangerous pack.
- **Planning:** place it where long target contact offsets its cost. Expose is most valuable when several other towers can attack the same target.

## Arena planning

### Foundry Loop

Use the central switchbacks for multi-segment coverage, then fill remote footprints with long-range towers. It is the cleanest arena for comparing tower roles because it has no Surge Nodes.

### Crosswind Basin

Opening coverage is deliberately exacting. Favor corners and positions that barely reach adjacent straight lanes; scattering cheap towers along single lanes wastes the limited early economy.

### Prism Circuit

The small number of build regions rewards compact completed roles and Beacon efficiency. Its three nodes are valuable but do not replace armor, shield, crowd, and control coverage.

### Surge Divide

The lower starting economy assumes early node use. Large nodes can geometrically fit four standard towers at roughly 24 units diagonally from the center when the surrounding build region permits it; smaller or clipped nodes may support only one or two. Fill useful node footprints with combat towers before adding unboosted expansion. A winning opening that ignores the node network is intentionally inefficient.

## Difficulty and directive expectations

- **Easy:** 90% enemy health, 98% speed, 112.5% starting credits, 24 lives, and a 30-wave campaign.
- **Medium:** authored health, speed, and economy with 20 lives and a 30-wave campaign.
- **Hard:** 112% health, 102% speed, 18 lives, and a 30-wave campaign.
- **Bastion:** Hard combat scaling, 16 lives, and the same complete 30-wave campaign.

Standard tests the complete toolset. Signal Gauntlet adds support carriers and tower disruption. Core Six is a roster puzzle rather than a universal numerical handicap. Entrenched tests permanent planning by removing tactical reserves, Protocols, selling, and the Forge.

## What the Experienced simulation agent represents

The Experienced policy is a deterministic planning heuristic, not machine learning and not a human win-rate predictor. It models practices an informed player is expected to discover:

- legal high-coverage placements and route corners;
- early node occupancy, including compact four-tower node patterns;
- non-redundant Signal Beacon coverage based on recipients that gain a stronger aura;
- scheduled Frost, armor, chain, shield, and durable-target counters;
- tower-specific targeting;
- a broad upgrade curve before excessive duplicate towers;
- late Apex reinvestment.

It still cannot improvise like a human, value a future sell/rebuild sequence perfectly, or learn a new strategy from a failed run. Simulation completion percentages are therefore comparative difficulty evidence. Final tower coordinates, branches, Apex state, and node occupancy are included in reports so surprising outcomes can be checked against actual layouts.

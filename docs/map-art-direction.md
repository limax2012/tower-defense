# Map art direction

Maximal Bastion depicts a municipal power-and-transit network under siege. Its four districts serve different parts of that infrastructure: heat production, elevated transport, virtual archives, and reactor containment. Shared graphite housings, replaceable ceramic armor, exposed service assemblies, and operational lighting connect these locations to the command deck and the defense hardware.

Working machinery establishes the world without additional readouts. Cyan identifies operational power, amber and orange convey industrial heat, violet belongs to virtual infrastructure, and magenta cores identify corrupted hostile machines. Helix retains its jade coolant and pale green reactor light. Health, shield, targeting, node, and placement overlays retain priority over environmental lighting.

## Materials and hardware

Route beds combine dark central surfaces with continuous district-colored edges. Build decks use shallow top-left lighting, fine seams, recessed grates or drains, quiet fasteners, and brighter corner brackets. Machinery uses offset shadows, inset cavities, edge highlights, collars, bolts, and localized emissive elements. Large light pools establish atmosphere while the brighter details remain concentrated around powered equipment.

Towers share a six-sided mounting socket with ceramic outriggers and mechanical attachments. A shallow lower rim and a compact, translucent shadow follow the socket's exact orientation; the upper-left light stays fixed as the weapon aims. Feathered shadow edges preserve the deck beneath them, and bounded offsets keep small interface icons and large catalog illustrations grounded at their respective scales. Their working assemblies distinguish their roles: exposed twin Needle rails, the Watchtower's long precision barrel and offset optic, the Breaker Cannon's broad braced breech and short heavy muzzle, the Shard Fan's spread of tubes, Frost Spire arms, an Ember Coil, Arc Relay electrodes, a recessed mortar bore, a framed Prism emitter, and the Signal Beacon antenna. Aiming, recoil, and power lights preserve recognizable silhouettes at battlefield and catalog scales.

Four recessed tier indicators stay fixed to the mounting plate as the weapon turns. Tier 1 leaves them dark; Tier 2 lights the upper pair of dots; Tier 3 also lights the short lower diagonal strips. Apex adds six separated power rails around the hexagonal socket in the weapon's energy color, with a slow glow pulse. Reduced Effects retains the lit tier markers and static Apex rails without bloom. Selection, ownership, and temporary combat statuses remain separate overlays.

Active protocols use one sparse perimeter of energy marks at a fixed distance from the tower. Shared brightness timing and weapon colors unify the effect; Needle ticks, Watchtower reticle marks, Frost facets, Shard fans, Ember flares, Breaker chevrons, Arc zigzags, Mortar charge bars, Prism triangles, and Beacon packets distinguish each weapon. These marks leave the core and tactical status rings clear. Reduced Effects freezes their motion and brightness and suppresses bloom while preserving the active-state silhouette. Brief activation bursts still show their gameplay area.

Weapon identity continues into flight through simple luminous forms, colored halos, and trailing light. Needle fires a short cyan streak; Watchtower fires a long, pale periwinkle lance; Shard Fan spreads small orange plasma beads; Frost Spire launches one bright ice-blue diamond; Ember Coil casts a red flame with a warm core; Breaker Cannon fires a broad gold chevron of compressed energy. Tower lighting, authored projectile colors, and impact effects use the same weapon palette. Burn shares Ember's red, and armor break shares Breaker's gold. Their core silhouettes remain distinct with Reduced Effects enabled. Arc Relay uses branching electrical links, while Prism Beam uses a focused beam.

Siege Mortar fires a large chartreuse plasma charge formed from soft overlapping light fields around a hot pale center. Its yellow-green bloom pulses roughly once per second, interior highlights circulate within the charge, and soft light packets drift backward through the diffuse wake. Its position and impact timing follow the simulation. Reduced Effects retains the compact luminous core with a fixed animation phase and suppresses the broad halo and wake.

Prism Beam retains a straight violet sheath and bright white optical core. Three tapered highlights travel along the beam during each firing flash, with subtle refraction at their edges and localized bloom at the emitter and impact. The emitter holds the shot's direction for the primary beam's 150 ms lifetime, then resumes normal aiming. The visible ray starts at the barrel tip using the same presented tower scale; its light fades with the ray. Chain links retain their enemy-to-enemy origins, and a fresh shot takes priority over an older fading primary ray. These are presentation rules and do not delay attacks or target selection. Reduced Effects retains the straight core and its normal lifetime fade while suppressing travelling highlights and broad glow. The animation does not alter damage timing or targeting.

Hostiles use articulated limbs or compact runner nacelles, split armor shells, and exposed magenta corruption cores. Heavy units carry larger segmented flank armor; Aegis adds shield emitters, Regenerators carry ribbed repair assemblies and rotating green repair rings, and bosses retain their gold threat markings around a magenta core. Status and health overlays remain independent of the mechanical artwork.

Arc Relay's stun appears as short cyan electrical filaments inside the hostile chassis, with a pale core and localized glow. Each discharge changes its length, angle, position, and branch point within the hull, then fades before the next flicker. Enemy-specific phases keep simultaneously stunned targets from flashing in unison. Its compact footprint leaves burn, shield, and outer status markings readable. Reduced Effects retains a steady cyan filament without flicker or bloom; the current disappears when stun expires.

## Layering and readability

Scenery is rendered below playable decks, paths, nodes, enemies, and towers. Decorative lights use lower opacity than targeting, health, and placement cues. Build surfaces keep their exact authored rectangular bounds; corner brackets and evenly spaced fasteners distinguish them from background props. Deck details are visual surface treatments, not placement obstacles. Route materials differ, but every route retains a continuous edge and follows its authored centerline and width.

Rainline has three clear layers: the bright continuous enemy causeway, bracketed defense decks, and a quiet lower utility plant. Every playable deck caps a solid machine housing projected 16 units right and 58 units down onto a foundation plinth. Deep cooling fins, armored rims, braced side walls, causeway piers, and cast shadows share this projection. Recessed paired-pipe galleries connect the housings and aligned cooling banks; their couplers and enclosed fans identify them as non-playable machinery. Narrow, subdued access walks connect nearby decks to the brighter enemy route. Deck ventilation, maintenance hatches, and drainage remain inside the legal build rectangles. Cyan utility lights cast faint wet reflections. Rain passes over the architecture but behind combat objects.

The environment never changes combat rules. Paths, build regions, restricted regions, Surge Nodes, starting resources, and wave sets are defined by map JSON. Display names may change independently of stable map IDs and path-style keys.

## Implementation

`Rendering/MapEnvironmentRenderer.cs` owns environment dispatch, reusable housing, pipe and route drawing, and map emblems. `Rendering/RainlineDistrictArt.cs` owns Rainline's lower utility plant, cooling housings, elevated supports, defense deck surfaces, and weather. `Rendering/DefenseTerminalArt.cs` carries the same district architecture into setup previews, with the authored route drawn in the foreground. `Rendering/NightGridArt.cs` owns the tower and hostile assemblies shared by the battlefield and tactical displays. `Rendering/TowerProtocolArt.cs` draws the shared perimeter treatment for active protocols. `Rendering/ProjectileArt.cs` and `Rendering/MortarShellArt.cs` draw weapon-specific flight shapes; existing source attribution preserves the appearance of shots from sold towers. `Rendering/PrismBeamArt.cs` draws the optical beam and its travelling light. These renderers use shared primitives and derive material colors from `Rendering/ColorPalette.cs`, which owns the four environment palettes. Map JSON mirrors authored ground and route colors; regression checks keep these aligned.

Moving center markings on every route share a continuous distance along the authored path. A marking spans both adjoining segments while crossing a corner, with fixed-width rounded ends keeping its contour continuous as it enters and leaves the turn.

Environmental animation reads session simulation time; combat artwork uses the local presentation frame. Neither mutates synchronized state. Reduced Effects fixes decorative animation phases at zero and suppresses decorative combat lighting while retaining the static identity of each map and machine. Hidden visual verification captures overview and combat scenes for every map, verifies ambient motion and its reduced-effects freeze, and checks that rendering leaves the session checksum intact.

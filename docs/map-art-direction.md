# Map art direction

Maximal Bastion depicts a municipal power-and-transit network under siege. Its four districts serve different parts of that infrastructure: heat production, elevated transport, virtual archives, and reactor containment. Shared graphite housings, replaceable ceramic armor, exposed service assemblies, and operational lighting connect these locations to the command deck and the defense hardware.

Working machinery establishes the world without additional readouts. Cyan identifies operational power, amber and orange convey industrial heat, violet belongs to virtual infrastructure, and magenta cores identify corrupted hostile machines. Helix retains its jade coolant and pale green reactor light. Health, shield, targeting, node, and placement overlays retain priority over environmental lighting.

## Materials and hardware

Route beds combine dark central surfaces with continuous district-colored edges. Build decks use shallow top-left lighting, fine seams, recessed grates or drains, quiet fasteners, and brighter corner brackets. Machinery uses offset shadows, inset cavities, edge highlights, collars, bolts, and localized emissive elements. Large light pools establish atmosphere while the brighter details remain concentrated around powered equipment.

Tower families use triangular, square, hexagonal, or octagonal mounting plates with ceramic supports and mechanical attachments. T1 is compact; T2 adds specialized supports; T3 gains advanced armor, emitters, or capacitor banks; Apex develops a larger family-specific assembly. A shallow lower rim and a compact, translucent shadow follow the socket's exact orientation; the upper-left light stays fixed as the weapon aims. Feathered shadow edges preserve the deck beneath them, and bounded offsets keep small interface icons and large catalog illustrations grounded at their respective scales. Their working assemblies distinguish their roles: exposed twin Needle rails, the Watchtower's long precision barrel and offset optic, the Breaker Cannon's broad braced breech and short heavy muzzle, the Shard Fan's exposed aperture and radial focusing petals, Frost Spire coolant banks, an Ember Coil, Arc Relay electrodes, a recessed mortar bore, a framed Prism emitter, and the Signal Beacon antenna. Directional emitters track targets. Shard, Frost, and Mortar upper assemblies turn steadily around central emitters; their foundations and tier lights remain fixed. Recoil and power lights preserve recognizable silhouettes at battlefield and catalog scales.

Four recessed tier indicators stay fixed to the mounting plate beneath rotating hardware. Tier 1 leaves them dark; Tier 2 lights two contacts; Tier 3 also lights the lower strips. Their positions follow the mount shape. Apex is distinguished by its final mechanical assembly. Reduced Effects retains structural differences and lit tier markers without bloom. Selection and ownership remain separate tactical overlays.

Operational conductors illuminate each family's barrel rails, cooling cradles, charge banks, electrodes, launch injectors, optical yokes, or antenna feeds. Restrained ambient energy and localized firing responses sit within dark structural housings. Active Protocols intensify these details and surround the entire machine with a broad, textured energy field beneath its model. Short flows above the model express the weapon technology: fast inward Needle charge, fine Watchtower convergence, outward Shard plasma, swirling Frost crystals, Ember heat, Breaker compression, branching Arc electricity, Mortar charge, split Prism rays, and paired Beacon signal packets. The field remains prominent throughout the active duration, with brief ignition and expiration transitions. Signal Beacons separately energize green receiving contacts on supported towers. Surge Nodes use their own colors in compact placement couplers and Tower Intel source labels. Saturated purple interruptions and small internal arcs identify disrupted circuits; orange aperture clamps identify suppressed output. Hardware lighting follows the tower's tier, scale, and aim; exterior flow remains centered on the machine. Reduced Effects fixes decorative phases and suppresses supplemental bloom while retaining powered materials and the active energy field.

Weapon identity continues into flight through simple luminous forms, colored halos, and trailing light. Needle fires a short turquoise streak; Watchtower fires a long neutral-white lance; Shard Fan spreads small orange plasma beads; Frost Spire launches one bright sky-blue diamond; Ember Coil casts a red flame with a warm core; Breaker Cannon fires a filled gold wedge with one connected white core. Arc Relay uses deep-blue electrical branches with bright white filaments, while Prism Beam uses a focused violet beam. Signal Beacon's emerald lighting and support receivers remain distinct from Needle's turquoise. Tower lighting, authored projectile colors, and impact effects use the same weapon palette. Burn shares Ember's red, and armor break shares Breaker's gold. Their core silhouettes remain distinct with Reduced Effects enabled.

Siege Mortar fires a large chartreuse plasma charge formed from soft overlapping light fields around a hot pale center. Its yellow-green bloom pulses roughly once per second, interior highlights circulate within the charge, and soft light packets drift backward through the diffuse wake. Its position and impact timing follow the simulation. Reduced Effects retains the compact luminous core with a fixed animation phase and suppresses the broad halo and wake.

Mortar impacts combine an intense contact flash with uneven, overlapping energy pockets and outward fragments. Pulse Plate detonations use the same pressure language in their own palette. Other weapon contacts express piercing sparks, orange plasma fragments, cold crystalline chips, red heat, gold armor splinters, branching current, or refracted violet light. Short jets and hot fragments reinforce Mortar and Ember muzzle discharge, while the shared white firing flash remains localized at the emitter. Enemy deaths release dark metallic debris with energized edges. Escape feedback contracts toward the departure point, and Forge completion draws short energy packets into the device.

Protocol ignition is a brief, family-colored release distinct from the sustained aura. Uneven propagation and localized light pockets carry area activations through the actual burst extent; frost chips, electrical forks, heat, and split optical rays identify the source. Compact activations remain near the machine, with inward intake for Needle and Beacon. These bursts use fragments and luminous volumes, while continuous lines remain reserved for tactical range boundaries. Reduced Effects reduces fragment counts and supplemental light while preserving flow, contact position, and effect lifetime.

Prism Beam retains a straight violet sheath and a luminous lavender optical core. The ray has a uniform cross-section with localized bloom at the emitter and impact, and fades smoothly during each firing flash. The emitter holds the shot's direction for the primary beam's 150 ms lifetime, then resumes normal aiming. The visible ray starts at the barrel tip using the same presented tower scale. The beam core grows with the tower's four hardware stages and fits inside the corresponding barrel housing. The violet sheath begins at full width at the barrel tip, with a rounded rear cap that wraps around the emitter without a rectangular edge; its light fades with the ray. The tower uses the shared white firing flash at its fixed barrel tip. The barrel housing and diamond border share the brand emblem's dark metal gray. The nested violet-and-white core uses concentric square geometry for straight, aligned edges at every aim angle and size. Chain links retain their enemy-to-enemy origins, and a fresh shot takes priority over an older fading primary ray. These are presentation rules and do not delay attacks or target selection. Reduced Effects retains the straight core and normal lifetime fade while suppressing the firing flash, outer sheath, and bloom. The animation does not alter damage timing or targeting.

Hostiles use graphite chassis, beveled gray armor, dark recesses, and magenta reactors. Runner is a small pointed interceptor; Crawler has four armored legs and a triangular reactor; Brute carries six legs around a hexagonal reactor; Aegis has eight legs and a larger octagonal shell; Regenerator is the largest machine, with a symmetric central reactor and twin tracks. Elite and Boss reinforcement preserves family construction. Grounded walking contacts use separate corridors and shorter corner steps; actual travel drives gait and tread motion in both effects modes. Silhouettes remain inside the road deck with a clean boundary margin.

Burn, Slow, Armor Break, and Expose use soft colored fog regions over the chassis. Full Effects blends drifting density layers; opacity transitions soften application and expiry. Stun instead uses thin branching Arc-blue discharges, contact sparks, and restrained chassis shudder while feet remain planted. The shield stays a whole, quiet stand-off shell while reserve remains. Native regeneration appears through health recovery without changing the symmetric model. Enemy condition details remain available in pinned Intel.

Visible enemies have fixed-width, closed-frame health and shield meters without hover enlargement. Signal gems are white, green, blue, orange, or saturated purple, with plain faceted faces. Complete signal bodies receive foreground priority, with normal occlusion between overlapping carriers. Accelerator, Restorer, and Bulwark outlines mark their application radii. Jammer sends orange wavefronts; Disruptor sends purple split-rail diamond pulses into matching tower fault channels. Disruption's saturated purple contrasts with Prism's lighter lavender violet. Reduced Effects retains gem identity and quieter transfer shapes.

## Layering and readability

Scenery is rendered below playable decks, paths, nodes, enemies, and towers. Decorative lights use lower opacity than targeting, health, and placement cues. Build surfaces keep their exact authored rectangular bounds; corner brackets and evenly spaced fasteners distinguish them from background props. Deck details are visual surface treatments, not placement obstacles. Route materials differ, but every route retains a continuous edge and follows its authored centerline and width. Small edge motifs stop outside the adjoining lane footprint at each turn; balanced spacing on the remaining straight run prevents clipped fragments or stray corner specks. Rainline uses centered edge dashes within the same clearance rule.

Moving route traces, hazard accents, and scenery bloom use subdued intensities so enemy cores, shields, and conditions carry greater contrast on all four district palettes. District colors and route geometry retain their authored values.

Rainline has three clear layers: the bright continuous enemy causeway, bracketed defense decks, and a quiet lower utility plant. Every playable deck caps a solid machine housing projected 16 units right and 58 units down onto a foundation plinth. Deep cooling fins, armored rims, braced side walls, causeway piers, and cast shadows share this projection. Recessed paired-pipe galleries connect the housings and aligned cooling banks; their couplers and enclosed fans identify them as non-playable machinery. Narrow, subdued access walks connect nearby decks to the brighter enemy route. Deck ventilation, maintenance hatches, and drainage remain inside the legal build rectangles. Cyan utility lights cast faint wet reflections. Rain passes over the architecture but behind combat objects.

The environment never changes combat rules. Paths, build regions, restricted regions, Surge Nodes, starting resources, and wave sets are defined by map JSON. Display names may change independently of stable map IDs and path-style keys.

## Implementation

`Rendering/MapEnvironmentRenderer.cs` owns environment dispatch, reusable housing, pipe and route drawing, and map emblems. `Rendering/RainlineDistrictArt.cs` owns Rainline's lower utility plant, cooling housings, elevated supports, defense deck surfaces, and weather. `Rendering/DefenseTerminalArt.cs` carries the same district architecture into setup previews, with the authored route drawn in the foreground. `Rendering/NightGridArt.cs` and its `TowerModelArt.cs` partial own tower assemblies shared by the battlefield and tactical displays. `Rendering/EnemyModelArt.cs` owns enemy family/rank armor, presentation sizes, and leg-contact geometry. `Rendering/EnemyModelContours.cs` supplies the shared material boundaries for models and status effects; their filled masks are cached on desktop and packed into the browser primitive atlas. `Rendering/TowerHardwareChannels.cs` supplies operational conductors and separate receiving contacts. `Rendering/TowerProtocolArt.cs` and `Rendering/TowerProtocolFields.cs` combine elevated machinery light, cached energy masks beneath models, and bounded family-specific flows above them, all driven by live Protocol timers. The field and impact density masks are initialized at renderer startup and shared with the browser primitive atlas. `Rendering/TowerStatusArt.cs` draws support and interference states; `Rendering/PowerNodeArt.cs` draws embedded node relays and placement couplers. `Rendering/EnemyStatusArt.cs` supplies durability effects; `Rendering/EnemyDebuffAuraArt.cs` and `Rendering/EnemyStunArt.cs` supply timed-condition visuals; `Rendering/EnemySignalArt.cs` supplies reactor identity, support boundaries, and recipient feedback. `Rendering/ProjectileArt.cs` and `Rendering/MortarShellArt.cs` draw weapon-specific flight shapes; existing source attribution preserves the appearance of shots from sold towers. `Rendering/PrismBeamArt.cs` draws the optical beam and its endpoint glow. These renderers use shared primitives and derive material colors from `Rendering/ColorPalette.cs`, which owns the four environment palettes. Map JSON mirrors authored ground and route colors; regression checks keep these aligned.

Moving center markings on every route share a continuous distance along the authored path. A marking spans both adjoining segments while crossing a corner, with fixed-width rounded ends keeping its contour continuous as it enters and leaves the turn.

Environmental animation reads session simulation time; combat artwork uses the local presentation frame. Neither mutates synchronized state. Reduced Effects uses fixed decorative animation phases and suppresses bloom while retaining powered materials and active-state silhouettes. Hidden visual verification captures overview and combat scenes for every map, verifies ambient motion and its reduced-effects freeze, and checks that rendering leaves the session checksum intact.
